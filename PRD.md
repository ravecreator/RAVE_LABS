# RAVE LABS
## Campus Transport
**Research, issues and product**  
**Team:** RAVE LABS  
**PROJECT NAME:**CAMPUSKEKS
**University:** Babcock University  
**Project Theme:** Campus transport  
**Phase:** Issue investigation and Product Planning

## 1. Introduction
As a software engineering group, we aim to investigate and analyse genuine issues students face. We then wish to understand current student approaches to these issues and to devise a suitable solution.

Whilst researching, campus transport emerged as a prominent issue. Students can face issues such as keke unavailability, long waiting times, extra charges and even inter-campus transport. A survey and supplementary research were undertaken to analyse this.

## 2. Background
Students require transport to attend classes, the cafeteria, hostels, administration, a teaching hospital and beyond. Despite keke transport being available, students state they often struggle to obtain this, particularly during busy periods.

This project wishes to:
- Identify key campus transport issues.
- Analyse how students manage around transport issues.
- Investigate transport availability, waiting times, charges and accessibility.
- Investigate why current transport provision may not fully meet students’ needs.
- Design a software-based product to address the issues, informed by the research.

## 3. Research type
A Google Forms survey was distributed to students across the various year groups and schools in the university. It analysed students’ main issues, their views on how these issues are addressed, how they manage around them and their reasons for considering that current addressing of these issues is inadequate.

70 people completed the survey. Again, transport was the most commonly nominated issue. Based upon this, RAVE LABS analysed transport more thoroughly. Further research was carried out concerning approximate keke numbers, transport demand, waiting times and charges together with students’ views on transport accessibility.

## 4. Survey results
### 4.1 Issues
| Issue | Responses | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Transport | 27 | 38.6% |
| Class management | 19 | 27.1% |
| Catering | 14 | 20.0% |
| Assignment management | 10 | 14.3% |
| **Total** | **70** | **100%** |

Students most commonly nominated transport as an issue. Next were class management, catering and assignment management. As a result, the group decided to analyse transport in greater depth.
### 4.2 Views on current addressing of issues
| Response | Number | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| No apparent resolution | 58 | 82.9% |
| Apparent resolution | 12 | 17.1% |

For the most part, students stated that they did not see any apparent resolution for their nominated issue. This represents students’ views and does not necessarily prove the university has no transport related measures in place.
### 4.3 Transport
Of the 27 students who nominated transport as their principle issue, 22 (81.5%) claimed there was no apparent transport resolution, and 5 (18.5%) said that there was. This re-enforced the decision to analyse transport further.

## 5. Further transport findings
Whilst students’ transport issues appear to go beyond keke unavailability, factors including transport availability, demand, waiting times, charges and transport choice could also play a part.
| Research point | Finding |
|---|---|
| Number of kekes estimated to be in operation | ~60 (unconfirmed) |
| Supply and demand | Supply appears to be outstripped by demand. |
| Area of issue | Transport issues generally occur throughout campus. |
| Estimated waiting time | Around 7 minutes, although this varies |
| Charge consistency | Charges reportedly differ according to destination. |
| Extra charge | Extra charges are stated to be incurred, notably for journeys to BUTH. |
| Transport choice | Students claim transport providers may favour particular routes according to their charge. |

The figure of 60 kekes has not been independently confirmed. Waiting times are also an estimate and may also depend on area and demand.

Students manage transport issues by walking/trekking, extra charging, waiting for alternative transport and/or changing location. Furthermore, students state they can sometimes struggle to be picked up for transport, especially for certain destinations. Again, these are students’ views. Operator interviews and direct observation would assist in analysing the reasons behind these issues.
## 6. Issues and analysis
Students generally face issues in relation to ease and reliability of obtaining keke transport.
| Issue | Student perspective |
|---|---|
| Availability | Struggling to obtain transport. |
| Waiting | Time spent waiting for transport. This differs depending on area and demand. |
| Charge | Extra charges and differing charges. |
| Choice | Perceived favouring of particular routes by transport providers according to charge. |
| Walking | Students at times trek in order to obtain transport. |
### Potential underlying reasons for issues
- It is possible that students require transport around the same time.
- Not all of the approximately 60 kekes may be in operation and/or accessible at suitable times.
- Transport may not be evenly distributed around campus.
- Transport providers may favour specific routes for reasons of charge and/or convenience.
- Students may be unaware of transport availability and/or waiting times.
- Students may be unaware of likely charges.

Students who utilise keke transport are primarily impacted by transport issues. Transport providers could also be impacted through gaining greater insight into students’ transport requirements and destinations. University management may also be impacted if collaboration and/or approval is required.
## 7. Current services and research constraints
Whilst a transport service is already in place, students state that they can find it challenging to access. The survey demonstrates that students struggle to obtain transport, face waiting times and pay differing charges. At present, it is yet to be confirmed whether there is a specific system in place to manage transport availability. Further analysis is required in this regard.

There are research constraints. Although 70 individuals took part in the survey, the sample may not fully represent the student body. Furthermore, many of those surveyed were 200 level students and so other year groups were under-represented. Neither the approximate number of kekes nor waiting times have been independently analysed. Although largely representing students’ views, detailed operator interviews have yet to be conducted and transport availability has not yet been analysed for different areas and times.
## 8. Solution
RAVE LABS intends to investigate the design of a service to aid students in identifying and accessing transport. Rather than seeking to replace current transport provision, the service will aim to facilitate students’ identification of transport and allow communication of destinations. Students will also be able to ascertain pick-up points.

Such a service would address:
| Current issue | Intended service enhancement |
|---|---|
| Difficulties in identifying transport | Improve transport identification |
| Uncertainty regarding transport availability | Display up-to-date transport availability |
| Uncertainty regarding charges | Present verified charge information |
| Difficulties in obtaining transport to certain destinations | Enable communication of destinations and transport availability |
| Insufficient transport information | Provide a central source of transport information |

Although these enhancements are proposed, they will need to be tested. There is no assurance that they will entirely prevent students from facing waiting times and transport unavailability.
## 9. Product objectives and MVP
### Objectives
- Facilitate students’ identification of transport.
- Decrease students’ uncertainty in relation to transport.
- Enhance students’ access to transport information including routes, destinations, pick-up points and charges.
- Design a simple, cost-effective and project time-frame appropriate product.
### MVP functionality
**Student functionality:**
- Access transport information.
- Identify/search for a destination.
- Ascertain transport for a nominated destination.
- Obtain charge information (where this is verified).
- Establish pick-up points.

**Operator functionality:**
- Access an operator interface.
- Define and amend availability.
- Alter a pick-up point.
- State destinations which they are prepared to travel to.

**The service should:**
- Display up-to-date transport availability and distinguish between available and unavailable transport.
- Present verified destination and charge information.
- Have an easily understood and navigable interface.
### Non-functional requirements
- **Usability:** The product should be easily comprehensible to those with diverse degrees of technical knowledge.
- **Performance:** Key functionality should be accessed swiftly.
- **Reliability:** Ideally, the product should display up-to-date transport availability.
- **Compatibility:** The product should function effectively on widely-used mobiles and web-browsers.
- **Security:** Student information should be secure and functionality should be restricted to authorised users.
- **Maintainability:** The product should be readily amendable and repairable by the group.
## 10. Users and requirements
### Users
- **Students (primary):** Identify, search for and access transport and information in relation to destinations.
- **Operators (secondary):** State transport availability, pick-up points and destinations.
- **University management (stakeholder):** Potentially becomes involved if collaboration and/or approval is required.
### Functional requirements
**Students should be able to:**
- Access the service.
- Identify/search for a destination.
- Ascertain transport for a nominated destination.
- Access charge information (where this is verified).
- Establish pick-up points.

**Operators should be able to:**
- Access an operator interface.
- Define and amend availability.
- Amend a pick-up point.
- State destinations which they are willing to travel to.

**The service must:**
- Display current transport availability and recognise available and unavailable transport.
- Provide verified destination and charge information.
- Have an easily understood and navigable interface.

Non-functional requirements (see above).
## 11. User stories and processes
**As a student I would like to:**
- Identify transport in order to reduce my search time.
- Search for a destination in order to identify relevant transport.
- Access charge information so that I can plan my expenditure.
- Ascertain pick-up points so that I can identify where to collect transport from.

**As an operator I would like to:**
- State my availability so that students can recognise when I am prepared to transport people.
- State my destinations and amend my availability so that students do not access obsolete information.

**Processes**
- Student: Access service → search for destination → identify transport → access pick-up and charge information → identify and enter transport.
- Operator: Access operator interface → define availability → search for destinations and/or routes → recognise interested students → undertake journey and amend availability.

These processes are provisional, and may alter during product development.
## 12. Evaluation criteria
Product evaluation will be undertaken via analysis of:
- Number of students who utilise the service (during testing).
- Accuracy of transport availability information.
- Whether students’ transport search time is reduced.
- Student satisfaction.
- Number of operators keen to engage with the service.
- Instances in which students successfully identify transport via the service.
## 13. Risks, assumptions and constraints
| Risk | Action to be taken |
|---|---|
| Low operator engagement | Design a simple operator interface and engage operators early. |
| Out-of-date transport availability | Enforce regular updating of transport availability and automatically discontinue previous information. |
| Incorrect charge information | Present solely verified charge information and explicitly denote estimated charges. |
| Poor internet connectivity | Design a light, data-efficient product. |
| Restricted development time | Focus on key MVP functionality. |
### Assumptions
- Students will be able to engage with the service via mobiles and/or other devices.
- Some operators may wish to engage with the service.
- Transport demand provides sufficient impetus to investigate a service to manage transport.
- Students may engage with the service if the information provided is both pertinent and accurate.
### Constraints
Time and resource availability. Verified transport information quantity. Inability to directly control operators. Reliance on operators to update information. And potential costs relating to service hosting and maintenance.
## 14. Future enhancements
Future service features could include real-time GPS tracking, anticipated arrival times, comparison of charges, queuing facilities, demand analysis, transport availability alerts and reporting of feedback and complaints. However, these will not form part of the MVP until it has been established that they are both required and achievable.
## 15. Conclusion
Following initial research, 70 students nominated transport as their most commonly identified issue. Research results and supplementary analysis demonstrated that students struggle to access transport, often face waiting times and pay differing charges. Moreover, students can also encounter issues in securing transport for particular destinations. RAVE LABS recognises that there is potential to further enhance students’ transport accessibility. However, it is vital that the devised solution meets genuine student needs and does not incorporate superfluous functionality. Going forward, the group will seek to further develop the service and determine the most crucial elements of the MVP. These will then be tested with students and operators engaged with the service.
